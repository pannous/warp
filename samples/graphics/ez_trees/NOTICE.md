# ez_trees

Seeded tree meshes read by lib/meshlets.warp (`read_tree_mesh`) for samples/graphics/ez_forest.warp and
samples/graphics/meshlet_grid.warp: pine, oak, birch, autumn and bush, each two levels of detail in the .eztree layout
(notes/meshlets.md "Tree meshes"). One tree height is 1; the base is at the origin, y up. The trunk stays on x = 0, z = 0.

Generated by scripts/ez_tree/export.mjs from EZ Tree by Daniel Greenheck, https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree,
commit dcf309bd86bd521083d9c70f01f2de45fdc7c457 (2026-07-16), its presets Pine Small, Oak Small, Aspen Small,
Ash Small and Bush 2 with their own seeds. Pine, oak, ash and bush keep that skeleton and use fewer leaves than the
preset, so the two levels stay small (the exporter's overrides). Meshed by Tree.createGeometry at the details of
Tree.defaultLODLevels[1] and [2]. EZ Tree Pro is not used.

`canopy/` is a second geometry set from the same presets and seeds, with more of each preset's leaves and four
details (scripts/ez_tree/export.mjs, the `canopy` argument). The two-level files above stay as the meshlet tests
pin them.

`hero/` is one full-detail level of Pine, Oak, Ash and Aspen Medium (scripts/ez_tree/hero.mjs), the presets the
demo draws, plus the geometry's own normals (`.nrm`, little-endian float triples in the same vertex order).
Pine Medium and Aspen Medium keep that preset's seed and skeleton and use more, larger leaf cards than the
published preset (pine 64 cards at size 2.4, aspen 40 at size 3.6), because the published cards read as a
thin crown. Oak Medium and Ash Medium are unchanged. Each species also has a `*_coarse.eztree` (leaf stride 3)
so the forest can draw more of the near row inside the triangle budget. `*_far.eztree` is the same skeleton
one card in eight (single-sided), which is the stride the million-tree view instances. The coarse and far files are geometry only. `samples/graphics/tree_study.warp` draws the full medium meshes.
`ash.rgba` and `grove.rgba` are raw RGBA atlases, top scanline
first, built by scripts/ez_tree/atlas.py from the textures below. The ash atlas is 1280×1024: the ash leaf at
(0, 0) in its own 1024×1024, Bark001 colour at (1024, 0) and its normal at (1024, 512), each 256×512. The grove
atlas is 2048×1024: pine, oak, ash and aspen leaves in 512×512 tiles across y = 0, then Bark001, Bark002 and
Bark003 colour with each normal just to its right, along y = 512.

`near.rgba` is the close-up atlas, 4096×1536: pine, oak, ash and aspen leaves at their own 1024, then
Bark001, Bark002 and Bark003 colour with each normal to the right, along y = 1024. A smaller grove leaf
is a premultiplied Lanczos resize, so the alpha stays soft. `atlas.bin` is the three images, RGBA8,
zlib-compressed (RFC 1950), so a run uploads the bytes instead
of rebuilding a float per byte. `atlas.txt` is the stamp: magic `EZAT`, version 1, the source hash, then each
entry's id, width and height. Id 1 is the grove atlas, id 2 the ash atlas, id 3 the near atlas. The hash is the sha256 of the
per-file sha256 hex lines of `atlas.sources`, joined by newlines. A run hashes those files and uses the bin
only when the stamp matches; otherwise it builds the floats from the `.rgba` files. Regenerate both with
`python3 scripts/ez_tree/atlas.py samples/graphics/ez_trees`. The pixels are the leaf images (MIT, above) and
the bark photographs (CC0, below), packed by that script.

## Textures

The leaf images in `textures/leaves/` (ash, aspen, oak, pine) are the ones bundled with the EZ Tree demo. Its
texture note says they are licensed under the project's own license, which is the MIT license above.

The bark images in `textures/bark/` are ambientCG photographs, CC0 1.0 (public domain). Attribution is not
required; it is recorded here the same way the demo records it. Ground, dirt and grass photographs from the
demo are not included: that texture note does not license them.
